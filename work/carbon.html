Carbon · Fintech

Rebuilding a mobile banking app around what users actually said

Carbon is a digital micro-finance banking app that makes financial services faster, cheaper and more convenient, available 24/7 from anywhere in the world.

Overview

One app, many financial products

Carbon houses a number of financial products. Users can open bank accounts and get loans, savings, airtime, TV subscriptions, bill payments, buy now pay later (BNPL) and debit cards. My role was to improve the experience across onboarding, payments, savings, cards, Carbon Zero, bill payments and airtime and subscription purchases.

Design decisions were driven by direct user research, not internal assumptions: interviews, usability testing with recruited users across device tiers, and documented findings shared with PMs and engineers.

Goal

The business wanted to increase the onboarding rate, disburse more loans and re-evaluate the whole app for a new interface and a better experience. It also wanted onboarding for new design hires to be easier, which meant an industry-standard design system and proper documentation: some screens live in the app didn't exist in Figma at all.

Problem

The interface didn't look modern or intuitive, some necessary features were missing, onboarding had many journey issues, and there was no in-app guidance for things like identity verification (KYC) requirements, so customer service was flooded with tickets. Navigating the app was painful.

The approach

Research, insight, impact

The Carbon team is agile. I focused on product design improvements, new feature requests, and a new interface and experience on the native iOS and Android apps, and contributed to the design system shared by a team of three designers.

Research varied with the problem. For Carbon Zero, a new product, I interviewed existing users and stakeholders. For the main app, I recruited three people to test new prototypes:

  • A tech-savvy user on a high-end iPhone
  • A less tech-savvy user on a mid-range iPhone
  • A non-tech-savvy user on a low-end Android phone

I ran sessions over Google Meet and documented everything in Confluence. We then met with PMs and engineers to discuss the issues raised and turn them into tickets.

What users told us

Onboarding pain points

We wanted more users to move up through KYC levels, which meant tasks like email verification and uploading a live selfie. These were the core pain points. Most have since been fixed.

1. A long registration process

"The registration process is too long, some screens can be merged, e.g. the email verification page. 6 pages for sign-up is too much."
"After entering my email and waiting for the confirmation email to drop, I get the welcome to Carbon screen. After clicking next it brings me back to the enter your email page."

2. A frustrating selfie upload

"Selfie page, the capture button disappears sometimes."
"After waiting for 5 mins, had to restart the app, then the selfie isn't saved any more." (covers face, sighing)
"Trying to capture a selfie now crashed the app. The app just keeps crashing."

3. Selfies failing on low-end Android phones

"I got a message that says 'picture quality not good enough, please retake a photo of you in a brighter area'. My phone camera still works. I live upstairs and I can't go out for sun just to take a selfie. I have tried like 10 times already. It's an old phone though."

4. The app freezing

"On first attempt, the verification email did not work. The app froze, I had to restart the app to finish the email verification."

Key insights

What changed in the redesign

  • Copy now focuses on benefits and is explicit about the value users get from signing up.
  • Stale screens were redesigned to stand up against close competitors such as Kuda.
  • New screens use animation to keep users engaged.
  • A progress bar shows users how long the process will take.
  • Icons now follow the updated design system, with better categorisation.
  • Android users no longer have to scroll all the way down to fill in certain KYC details.
  • Short notes explain why we ask for employment and personal details, to build trust.

Outcome

Released in stages, all of it live

Design work shipped in stages, and everything in this case study has gone live. The Carbon app is available on iOS and Android.

  • Over 30% increase in sign-up rate.
  • Android Play Store rating went from 3.9 to 4.3.
  • More organised design system practices and easier collaboration with engineers.
